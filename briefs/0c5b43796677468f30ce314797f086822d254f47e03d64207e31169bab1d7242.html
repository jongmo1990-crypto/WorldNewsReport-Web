BOJ July Outlook: Above-Target Inflation Into FY2026

This is the BOJ's own semi-annual economic and price outlook, a primary input into Japan's monetary policy trajectory. A forecast of inflation clearly above 2% through H2 FY2026 -- driven partly by structural AI-linked semiconductor demand and yen weakness rather than purely by transitory oil effects -- reinforces (without confirming) the case for continued BOJ policy normalization, which matters for JPY, JGB yields, and globally for yen-funded carry trades. It also reaffirms the BOJ's own read that AI-related demand is a live, structural growth and price driver for Japan, distinct from the separately flagged Middle East oil-price risk.

자세히 보기

① 무슨 일이 있었나

The Bank of Japan released the "Highlights of the Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices (July 2026)" on August 17, 2026 (OFFICIAL/PRIMARY source). Per the report's own stated text, Japan's economy is projected to continue growing moderately but at a decelerating pace, supported by rising AI-related demand and government measures, while restrained by higher crude oil prices tied to the Middle East situation. Headline y/y CPI is projected to be clearly above 2% from the second half of FY2026, driven by higher semiconductor and other prices (reflecting AI-related demand), yen depreciation, and elevated crude oil prices; as the oil-price effect fades, inflation is expected to move toward around 2% through FY2028. Underlying (core) CPI inflation is expected to rise gradually toward a level generally consistent with the 2% price stability target. The report flags the Middle East situation, AI-related demand developments, and foreign-exchange developments as key risks, noting a risk that underlying CPI could deviate upward above the 2% target (the evidence excerpt is truncated beyond this point).
